# Intelligence Distillation: Witness Protection Act (112 of 1998)

## 🛡️ Core Mandate
The **Witness Protection Act 112 of 1998** provides the statutory framework for the protection of witnesses and related persons in South Africa. It establishes the **Office for Witness Protection (OWP)** within the Department of Justice.

## 🔑 Key Provisions & Powers

### 1. The Office for Witness Protection (OWP)
*   **Establishment (Sec 2)**: A dedicated office within the Department of Justice.
*   **Director's Access (Sec 10.2)**: The Director has "immediate and full access to any police docket and statement of a witness." This is a critical intelligence-gathering power that bypasses standard police hierarchy.
*   **Secrecy (Sec 17)**: Members of the Office must take an oath of confidentiality. Disclosure of protected information is an offence punishable by up to **30 years imprisonment** (Sec 22.1).

### 2. The Application Protocol
*   **Eligibility (Sec 7)**: Any witness who believes their safety is threatened can apply via a police station, prison official, public prosecutor, or directly to the OWP.
*   **Temporary Protection (Sec 8)**: Provides for 14 days of "emergency" protection while the application is evaluated.
*   **Protection Agreement (Sec 11)**: A binding contract where the witness agrees to:
    *   Give evidence in the proceedings.
    *   Refrain from criminal activities.
    *   Accept reasonable requests from the Office.

### 3. The Schedule of Offences
The OWP is mandated to protect witnesses in high-stakes cases, including:
*   **Organized Crime**: Any offence referred to in the Prevention of Organised Crime Act (POCA).
*   **Systemic Corruption**: Offences under the Prevention and Combating of Corrupt Activities Act (PRECCA) involving amounts > R10,000 or syndicates.
*   **Official Corruption**: Any offence committed by a **law enforcement officer**.
*   **Violent Crime**: Treason, Murder, Rape, and Robbery with aggravating circumstances.

| **Governance (Sec 3)** | The Director is appointed by the Minister of Justice, creating potential political influence over which witnesses are prioritized. |

## 🔄 Evolutionary Gap: 1998 vs. 2026 Reforms
*   **Incentives**: The 1998 Act is purely defensive (safety-oriented). It lacks the **15-25% financial incentive** model proposed in the 2026 reforms, which shifts the paradigm from "protection" to "compensation for truth."
*   **Governance Crisis**: The "Dual Home" friction between the NPA and DOJCD remains the primary structural bottleneck. The 1998 Act places the OWP firmly within the DOJCD (Justice), whereas the NPA (Prosecution) is the primary "consumer" of the witness's testimony.
