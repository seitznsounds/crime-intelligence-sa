# Forensic Intelligence Distillation: Protection of Information Act 1982

## 🛡️ Metadata
- **Act No**: 84 of 1982
- **Commencement**: 16 June 1982
- **Context**: Apartheid-era "Official Secrets" legislation, still active with modern amendments.
- **Vulnerability Status**: Extreme (Weaponized against transparency)

## 🎯 Intelligence Points

### 1. The "Whistleblower Trap" (Section 4)
- **The Offence**: Criminalizes the possession and unauthorized disclosure of ANY document or information which a person "knows or reasonably should know" relates to security matters or is kept in a prohibited place.
- **Coercion Vector**: This section is used to threaten whistleblowers with 10 years of imprisonment for exposing state capture, under the guise that the evidence (e.g., a leaked contract or a police docket) is "secret official information."

### 2. Presumptions of Guilt (Section 8, 9, 10) - [SYSTEMIC INJUSTICE]
- **Reversed Burden of Proof**: If a person is found to have communicated with an "agent" (broadly defined), it is **legally presumed** they intended to disclose information for a purpose prejudicial to the security of the Republic.
- **Radical Logic**: The accused must prove their innocence (lack of intent), which is a direct violation of standard democratic legal principles but remains a cornerstone of SA "security" law.

### 3. Prohibited Places (Section 2 & 14)
- **Broad Definitions**: Includes any place used for building/repairing armaments or any area declared by the President.
- **Sanction**: Up to 20 years for merely being "in the neighbourhood" of a prohibited place with "prejudicial intent" (again, presumed).

### 4. Trials Behind Closed Doors (Section 13)
- **Transparency Block**: Allows courts to hold trials in camera (secret) for "security matters."
- **Implication**: High-profile corruption trials involving intelligence agencies or the SADF can be effectively hidden from public scrutiny using this provision.

### 5. Weaponization of "Security Matter"
- **Scope**: Includes anything dealt with by the State Security Agency (SSA).
- **Abuse Vector**: During the "State Capture" era, the definition of security matters was expanded to include purely political activities, allowing for the illegal surveillance and intimidation of activists under the color of law.
