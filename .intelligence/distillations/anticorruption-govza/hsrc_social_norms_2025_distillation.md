## 🎯 Executive Summary
The most comprehensive social audit of South African corruption attitudes to date. It identifies a "Reporting Shift" where willingness to report has jumped by 10% (to 46%), yet 62% of citizens remain paralyzed by fear of retaliation. It codifies the "Social Norms" of corruption, specifically the **RoCoSN Index** (Codes of Silence) and the normalization of **Sextortion** (41% exposure).

## 🚩 Institutional & Social Findings

### 1. The "Radius of Codes of Silence" (RoCoSN Index)
*   **Finding**: 34% of South Africans adhere to strong codes of silence, viewing reporting as a "moral betrayal" of their social network.
*   **Target Groups**: Strongest among public sector workers, trade union members, and residents of Western Cape/KZN/North West.

### 2. Workplace Culture Toxicity
*   **Counter-intuitive Finding**: Seeking advice from workplace mentors is correlated with *higher* tolerance for corruption.
*   **Implication**: Workplace mentorship in the public sector often serves to *socialize* new recruits into corrupt norms rather than ethical ones.

### 3. The Role Model Deficit
*   **17% of adults** have NO role model for ethical conduct.
*   **Mandela** remains a primary public role model, but family (44%) is the only resilient ethical anchor.
