BRICS ANTI-CORRUPTION WORKING GROUP
Questionnaire on Asset Recovery
Background
The BRICS countries have acknowledged on multiple occasions that illicit financial flows and
ill-gotten wealth stashed in foreign jurisdictions are a global challenge that negatively affects
economic growth and sustainable development. This is why enhanced cooperation in asset
recovery is a long-standing priority for BRICS. Over the years, BRICS have undertaken multiple
initiatives in this regard, including the launch of the BRICS Expert Network on Asset Recovery
in 2018, the expert meeting on asset recovery in 2019, and the Initiative on Denial of Safe Haven
to Corruption in 2022. Despite the progress countries have made in the area, there is still space
for improvement and closer interaction.
Furthermore, asset recovery remains a persistent challenge, with only 1% of proceeds of crime
recovered, according to some estimates1. This indicates that the existing mechanisms are not
effective enough, and more robust instruments are needed to assist countries in the identification,
disruption, prevention and fight against the transfer of illicit property. In this context, the
upcoming special session of the Conference of the States Parties to the United Nations
Convention against Corruption (CoSP) to be tentatively held after 2026 may be an important
occasion to explore all possible ways for improving the existing international asset recovery
frameworks. The BRICS countries should therefore consider defining common challenges in
asset recovery and identifying ways for eliminating the existing barriers.
Against this background, the objective of the present questionnaire is to promote an initial
overview of the domestic regulations on and the organisational setting for asset recovery in
BRICS countries, the scope of their participation in international cooperation in the area and the
instruments they use, as well as associated challenges and suggestions on how to address them.
The information gathered will be used to enrich the BRICS Common Position Document on
Enhancing the Effectiveness of Anti-Corruption Cooperation and Asset Recovery to be drafted
and adopted by the BRICS Anti-Corruption Working Group (ACWG) in 2024 and inform the
discussion of the issue in the ACWG and BRICS thematic events also in terms of ideas to be
promoted in the CoSP special session on asset recovery.
Countries are invited to provide, on a voluntary basis, condensed answers to the following
questions. It is also possible to skip certain questions, if there is no information requested or it is
preferable not to share it. At the same time, any further information on the topic, if deemed
valuable for the purposes of this questionnaire, would be highly appreciated.

2.9 In South Africa two distinct process are allowed for in terms of asset forfeiture law and
the procedure to manage frozen or seized assets:
- Instances where a curator bonis is appointed by a High Court; and
- Instances where the property is safeguarded by a 3rd party, such as a banking institution.
Matters wherein curators bonis are appointed
2.10 In terms of the provisions of POCA, a High Court may appoint a curator bonis, on
application by the National Director, to protect and safe guard frozen assets pending finalisation
of confiscation or forfeiture proceedings.
2.11 The appointment of a curator is made by the Court in the exercise of an independent
discretion. The relevant sections of POCA reads as follows:
In respect of criminal forfeiture (Chapter 5 of POCA)
2.12 Section 28 of POCA deals with the appointment of a curator bonis, by a High Court, in
respect of property subject to a restraint order and reads as follows: ‘(1) Where a High Court has
made a restraint order, that court may at any time –
(a) appoint a curator bonis to do, subject to the directions of that court, any one or more of
the following on behalf of the person against whom the restraint order has been made, namely –
(i) to perform any particular act in respect of any of or all the property to which the restraint
order relates;
(ii) to take care of the property;
(iii) to administer the said property; and
(iv) where the said property is a business or undertaking, to carry on, with due regard to any
law which may be applicable, the business or undertaking;
(b) order the person against whom the restraint order has been made to surrender forthwith,
or within such period as that court may determine, any property in respect of which a curator
bonis has been appointed under paragraph (a), into the custody of that curator bonis.’
In respect of civil forfeiture (Chapter 6 of POCA)
2.13 Section 42 of POCA deals with the appointment of a curator bonis, by a High Court, in
respect of property subject to a preservation of property order and reads as follows: ‘(1) Where a
High Court has made a preservation of property order, the High Court shall, if it deems it
appropriate, at the time of the making of the order or at a later time–
(a) appoint a curator bonis to do, subject to the directions of that High Court, any one or
more of the following on behalf of the person against whom the preservation of property order
has been made, namely –
(i) to assume control over the property;
(ii) to take care of the property;
(iii) to administer the said property and to do any act necessary for that purpose; and
(iv) where the said property is a business or undertaking, to carry on, with due regard to any
law which may be applicable, the business or undertaking; and
(b) order any person holding property subject to the preservation of property order to
surrender forthwith, or within such period as that Court may determine, any such property in
respect of which a curator bonis has been appointed under paragraph (a), into the custody of the
curator bonis.’
